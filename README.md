# $\phi^4$ Kink and Anti-Kink Project

## Project Overview

This project investigates the analytical and numerical properties of topological solitons in the $\phi^4$ field theory.

We begin by deriving the static analytical solutions corresponding to a kink and an anti-kink. We then implement numerical finite difference methods to solve the governing nonlinear partial differential equation $PDE$, examining the accuracy and stability of the numerical scheme.

The numerical model is then used to investigate moving kinks. We compare the simulated solutions with the corresponding Lorentz-transformed analytical solutions to assess how accurately the numerical method reproduces relativistic motion.

Finally, we study kink–antikink collisions, investigating how their dynamics depend on the initial collision velocity. In particular, we examine the different behaviours that can emerge, including scattering, annihilation and the formation of long-lived oscillatory states.

## Project Objectives

1. **Analytical solutions** — Derive the static kink and anti-kink solutions of the $\phi^4$ field equation.
2. **Numerical implementation** — Develop a finite difference scheme to solve the nonlinear PDE and investigate its numerical stability and accuracy.
3. **Moving kinks** — Simulate kinks travelling at constant velocity and compare the results with their exact Lorentz-transformed analytical solutions.
4. **Collision dynamics** — Simulate kink–antikink collisions across a range of initial velocities and investigate the resulting behaviour.
5. **Analysis and visualisation** — Present the numerical results through plots, animations and mathematical discussion.
